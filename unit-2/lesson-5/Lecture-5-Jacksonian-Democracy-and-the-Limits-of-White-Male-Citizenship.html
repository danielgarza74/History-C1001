Jacksonian Democracy and the Limits of White Male Citizenship
 
This lecture runs over two days. Follow the slide show and record your thinking in your Lecture Notes 5 packet. Keep one Central Question in mind: How did Andrew Jackson both expand and limit democracy, and what were the consequences of his presidency for the Union, for Indigenous nations, and for the national economy?

Directions
1.Get ready. Open the slide show using the button above and have your Lecture Notes 5 packet in front of you. Fill in your name, class/period, and date.

3.Complete every activity box as it appears: Where Do You Stand? (before position 5), the Power Tracker (positions 10–12), the pair activities (judgment, explanation, ranking), and the Day 1 Exit Ticket.
4.Day 1 jigsaw prep. Find your group’s slide and preview its pages. Write the main idea in one sentence, choose two details, and prepare one question to ask the class.
5.Day 2 warm-up. Answer the retrieval check from memory, then check your Day 1 notes and correct what you missed.
6.Source First. Study King Andrew the First before position 23, then test the artist’s claim against positions 23–25.
7.Jigsaw. Groups A–E teach positions 27, 28, 29, 31, and 32 (about 3 minutes each). As each group teaches, record the group letter, the main claim, and two pieces of evidence.
8.Day 2 Exit Ticket. Choose one synthesis question (slide 30 or slide 33) and answer with a claim, two specific pieces of evidence, and one qualification.
